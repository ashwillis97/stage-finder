What’s On In Blackpool? Music and Lots of it!

24 December 2025

6 min read

Share

News
24 December 2025

6 min read

3292 views

Share this article

Everyone’s asking, What’s On In Blackpool? Music and Lots of it! says Blackpool’s Grand Theatre, which is delighted to announce its biggest lineup in music ever!

There’s something reassuringly defiant about great pop songs refusing to fade quietly into the past. They linger. They soundtrack lives. They demand to be heard live, loud and shared. Over the next 18 months, Blackpool Grand Theatre is leaning fully into that idea, programming a run of music-led shows that treats pop history not as nostalgia, but as living culture, led by two full-scale British musicals that understand exactly why these songs still matter.

 

“Music brings people together.”

– Madonna

 

First up is Sunny Afternoon, Ray Davies’ wry, sharp-edged love letter to The Kinks, playing Tuesday 28 April to Saturday 2 May. This isn’t a tribute act in the pub sense of the word; it’s a proper, Olivier Award-winning musical that charts the band’s rise through the grit, glamour and bloody-minded brilliance of 60s Britain. The songs land because the story behind them is just as combustible – family tensions, class anxiety, sudden fame and that uniquely British knack for biting satire wrapped in melody. Hearing You Really Got Me or Waterloo Sunset in a theatre like Blackpool Grand feels less like revival and more like cultural reclamation.

 

 

Later in the summer comes Summer Holiday, running from Wednesday 29 July to Saturday 8 August, a sun-soaked jukebox musical built around the irrepressible run of hits made immortal by Cliff Richard and The Shadows. On paper, it’s escapism – scooters, Europe, young love – but beneath the charm is a reminder of a moment when British pop was learning how to export optimism to the world. Big choruses, clean harmonies and an infectious sense of momentum make this one a genuine crowd-pleaser, not just a memory lane stroll.

Around these two anchors, The Grand’s music programme reads like a lovingly curated mixtape, stretching from operatic heartbreak to synth-pop euphoria. The Roy Orbison Story arrives on Sunday 25 January, celebrating a voice that could break hearts in three notes flat, followed by The Wonder of Stevie on Sunday 15 February, a night devoted to Stevie Wonder’s peerless run of soul, funk and pop that still defines what “timeless” actually means.

There’s pop royalty of a different stripe when Jason Donovan takes the stage on Tuesday 10 March, before the unapologetic hit parade of The Ultimate Girl Band Show on Thursday 12 March. From there, The Grand swings back through American doo-wop and early rock’n’roll with Lipstick On Your Collar on Sunday 12 April, then straight into 80s pop heaven as WHAM! The Show lands on Sunday 19 April.

The programme doesn’t pretend pop stopped evolving. K-Pop Live on Sunday 26 Apriltaps into the global phenomenon reshaping modern fandom, while classic stadium rock is honoured by The Sound of Springsteen on Sunday 10 May and The Sensational 60s Experience on Friday 22 May, reminding us just how much cultural ground was covered in a single decade.

 

“One good thing about music, when it hits you, you feel no pain.”

– Bob Marley

 

Summer 2026 keeps the tempo high. The Illegal Eagles (Sunday 31 May), disco juggernaut Lost In Music (Thursday 4 June), The Carpenters Story (Sunday 21 June) and Think Floyd (Sunday 28 June) roll through, before the neon blast of 80’s Live on Thursday 9 July and Elton John celebration The Rocket Man on Friday 17 July.

 

 

As autumn rolls in, the focus turns to harmony and heritage. The Upbeat Beatles (Sunday 6 September), Love Hurts (Sunday 13 September), Ore Oduba fronting The Vintage Boys Rock ’n’ Roll Show (Tuesday 29 September) and bombastic balladry via Anything for Love (Saturday 3 October) all make their mark. Musical theatre crossover fans are catered for with Dreamcoat Stars on Sunday 11 October, while The Phantoms starring Lee Mead arrives on Sunday 25 October.

Love Hurts

 

The year closes with singalong staples and northern pride: Hello Again… The Neil Diamond Songbook (Wednesday 25 November) and Northern Live (Saturday 28 November), before ABBA’s indestructible catalogue brings things full circle with Thank You For The Music on Sunday 24 January 2027.

What ties this season together isn’t nostalgia for its own sake, but respect for the songs, and the audiences who know exactly why they still hit home. In a room like The Grand, with its history and its acoustics, these shows aren’t background noise. They’re events.

 

“The audience is the other half of the performance.”

– Paul McCartney

 

At its best, music doesn’t just entertain, it reconnects. It pulls old memories into the present tense, stirs emotions you didn’t realise you’d packed away, and reminds you who you were when these songs first found you. Whether it’s the sting of a lyric, the rush of a chorus or the collective lift of a room singing in unison, live music has a way of cutting through the noise and landing straight on the soul. This season at Blackpool Grand Theatre is an invitation to feel all of that again, not through headphones or playlists, but together, in a shared space where these songs can breathe properly.

Tickets for all shows are on sale now at blackpoolgrand.co.uk or via the Box Office (Church Street) – because some music deserves to be heard live, and some nights deserve to be remembered.
